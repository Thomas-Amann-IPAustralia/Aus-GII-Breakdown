# Evidence register

Compiled from `data/raw/evidence/register.yaml` by `corpus run evidence`; edit the YAML, not this file.
Journal items are checked against Crossref (DOI shown where the title matched).

## Report

- Productivity Commission (2016). Intellectual Property Arrangements, Inquiry Report No. 78. https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries/completed/intellectual-property/report Questions 1,4. Australia's most thorough review of the IP system's costs and benefits, and the source of the innovation patent's abolition.
- The Treasury (2023). Measuring What Matters, Australia's first wellbeing framework. https://treasury.gov.au/policy-topics/measuring-what-matters Questions 1,5. The government's own definition of the outcomes public value is measured against (the `mwm` source holds its indicators).

## Study

- Schankerman, M. and Pakes, A. (1986). Estimates of the value of patent rights in European countries during the post-1950 period. *The Economic Journal*. https://doi.org/10.2307/2233173 Questions 1,3. The renewal-based method for the private value of patents that IPGOD's renewals and lapses allow for Australia.
- Farre-Mensa, J., Hegde, D. and Ljungqvist, A. (2020). What is a patent worth? Evidence from the U.S. patent "lottery". *The Journal of Finance*. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12867 Questions 1,3,5. Uses examiner leniency to estimate what a first patent adds to a startup's growth and funding; the design that internal examination data would allow here.
- Williams, H. L. (2013). Intellectual property rights and innovation: evidence from the human genome. *Journal of Political Economy*. https://doi.org/10.1086/669706 Questions 1. Finds IP on genes reduced follow-on scientific research and product development.
- Sampat, B. and Williams, H. L. (2019). How do patents affect follow-on innovation? Evidence from the human genome. *American Economic Review*. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20151398 Questions 1. Using examiner leniency, finds gene patents did not on average reduce follow-on innovation, qualifying Williams (2013).
- Galasso, A. and Schankerman, M. (2015). Patents and cumulative innovation: causal evidence from the courts. *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qju029 Questions 1. Patent invalidation raises follow-on citations, mainly when large firms' patents block small innovators.
- Ouellette, L. L. (2012). Do patents disclose useful information?. *Harvard Journal of Law & Technology*. Questions 1. Survey evidence on whether disclosure, a public-value channel in section 2 of the data plan, actually informs other researchers.
- Graham, S. J. H., Merges, R. P., Samuelson, P. and Sichelman, T. (2009). High technology entrepreneurs and the patent system: results of the 2008 Berkeley Patent Survey. *Berkeley Technology Law Journal*. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.1429049 Questions 2,3. Why startups patent (finance, protection from copying) and why many do not (cost).
- Jensen, P. H. and Webster, E. (2006). Firm size and the use of intellectual property rights. *Economic Record*. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-4932.2006.00292.x Questions 2. Australian evidence on how SMEs' use of patents, trade marks and designs differs from large firms'.
- Webster, E., Jensen, P. H. and Palangkaraya, A. (2014). Patent examination outcomes and the national treatment principle. *The RAND Journal of Economics*. https://doi.org/10.1111/1756-2171.12053 Questions 2. Whether patent offices treat domestic and foreign applicants alike; relevant to who benefits from IP Australia's decisions.
- Hegde, D., Ljungqvist, A. and Raj, M. (2022). Quick or broad patents? Evidence from U.S. startups. *The Review of Financial Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhab097 Questions 3,4. Faster examination helps startups grow and raise funds; examination timeliness is a lever IP Australia controls.

## Review

- Moser, P. (2013). Patents and innovation: evidence from economic history. *Journal of Economic Perspectives*. https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.27.1.23 Questions 1. Historical evidence that patents shift the direction of innovation more than they raise its rate.
- Boldrin, M. and Levine, D. K. (2013). The case against patents. *Journal of Economic Perspectives*. https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.27.1.3 Questions 1. The strongest statement of the view that patents' net contribution may be negative, to be tested rather than assumed.
- Bloom, N., Van Reenen, J. and Williams, H. (2019). A toolkit of policies to promote innovation. *Journal of Economic Perspectives*. https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.3.163 Questions 4,5. Compares the evidence on R&D tax credits, research funding, skills, competition and IP as innovation levers.
- Hall, B., Helmers, C., Rogers, M. and Sena, V. (2014). The choice between formal and informal intellectual property: a review. *Journal of Economic Literature*. https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.52.2.375 Questions 3. When firms choose secrecy, lead time or complexity over registration, and what follows.
- Frijters, P. and Krekel, C. (2021). A Handbook for Wellbeing Policy-Making. *Oxford University Press*. https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192896803.001.0001 Questions 5. Methods and parameters for wellbeing cost-benefit analysis.
