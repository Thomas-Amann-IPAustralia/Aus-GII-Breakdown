# 0042: Policy breaks are read by kind, with controls; fee breaks by direction; later additions carried into §5 to §8

**Date:** 2026-11-02. **Whose:** the owner chose the theme (A27: later additions not carried back in §5 to §8, with
policy and fees); Claude chose how. The findings acted on, and the check of each, are in
`analysis/GPT-audit/261030/AMENDMENTS.md` (pass 7).

**Context.** The full Stage 2 audit (`analysis/claude-audit/261102/`) found that §5 to §8, which no earlier audit had
covered, still carried statements that later additions had overtaken (the fee history said to be missing, the
crosswalk with services said to be to come, "every companion table screened"), and that §5's policy and fee breaks
were read further than the placebo design allows. What the checks showed:
1. **Most policy breaks are certain by law or processing.** Expiries must stop when the term lengthens, applications
   when a right closes, extensions when a regime begins; registrations pause at a new Act. A placebo comparison
   confirms these, and says nothing of behaviour. All four candidate steps, not three, are beyond every placebo in
   some series.
2. **No control series had been tested.** Tested now: quiet around Raising the Bar and the Designs Act; but
   Australians' designs stepped down beyond every placebo when the innovation patent closed, and design registrations
   at the Patents Act 1990, so those windows hold more than the change.
3. **The 20-year term's own window is incomplete** (6 of 12 months before; the expiry series starts in January 1995),
   while every placebo's must be complete.
4. **Fee breaks were counted without direction.** Of five pairs beyond 95% of placebos, one points the way a price
   would (Australians' designs after July 1988), which B47 had left out; the 1998 rush, read as price-like, is not.
5. **Dependence.** A change's Australian and overseas pairs share its cost; placebo windows overlap.

**Decision.**
1. **Each policy break is classified** (mechanical, administrative, behavioural) and read only for what it can
   identify, never as a natural experiment by itself; each is set beside two control series.
2. **An event's window is reported with its months before,** so an incomplete window is visible.
3. **Fee breaks are named one by one,** with their sign and whether they point the way a price would.
4. **Placebo shares are ranks among overlapping windows,** never p-values; bootstrap intervals resample changes, not
   pairs.
5. **Later additions:** every observation that a later source or section overtook carries a pointer to it, with a
   dated restatement (as ADR 0038); §8 says what was screened, what was examined as selected pairs and what has no
   Stage 2 treatment (the PBS series, deferred with the medicine case study, OQ8); the lens summaries' carried signals
   appear in §8's list (B15, B62).
6. **B43 states its scope:** each indicator's period, the sources left out, and the patent column repeated through
   the crosswalk with services under its own correction.

**Consequences.** No lens changes; `screens.yaml`'s timeline reasons cite the current sections and the PBS's deferral.
B37's reading narrows (Raising the Bar's rush, and perhaps the Designs Act's overseas step, are the behavioural
breaks); B47's "the clearest breaks are new Acts" stands, with one price-like break added; B48's interval widens a
little (-0.638 to 0.049); B43 gains two passes through the crosswalk with services, still a single sample.
